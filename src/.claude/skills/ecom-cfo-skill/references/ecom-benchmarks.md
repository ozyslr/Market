### Diagnostic Decision Tree

```
START: Is profit margin > 5%?
│
├── NO: Is gross margin > 30%?
│   ├── NO: Is COGS > 30%?
│   │   ├── YES → Problem: Sourcing costs too high
│   │   │   Action: Renegotiate supplier, find alternatives, raise price
│   │   └── NO: Is fulfillment > 25%?
│   │       ├── YES → Problem: Fulfillment inefficiency
│   │       │   Action: Review packaging, shipping method, channel (FBA vs FBM vs 3PL)
│   │       └── NO → Problem: Revenue leakage (returns, discounts, fees)
│   │           Action: Audit return rate, discount strategy, fee structure
│   │
│   └── YES: Is ad spend > 15%?
│       ├── YES → Is contribution margin > 20%?
│       │   ├── NO → Problem: Ads eating margin
│       │   │   Action: Cut ad spend, improve organic rank, review ACOS by SKU
│       │   └── YES → Problem: Fixed costs too high
│       │       Action: Cut OpEx. "$3K in opex cuts = $32K+ in sales equivalent"
│       └── NO → Problem: Combination of cost pressures
│           Action: Run 4-metric analysis on all SKUs, find the bleeders
│
└── YES: Is contribution margin > 25%?
    ├── YES → Business is healthy
    │   Action: Optimize, scale winners, prune bottom 20%
    └── NO → Margin compression in progress
        Action: Check trend direction. If declining, diagnose before it hits <5%
```

## 5. Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)

### Formula
```
CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO

Where:
  DIO = Days Inventory Outstanding = (Avg Inventory ÷ COGS) × 365
  DSO = Days Sales Outstanding = (Avg Accounts Receivable ÷ Revenue) × 365
  DPO = Days Payable Outstanding = (Avg Accounts Payable ÷ COGS) × 365
```

### Ecommerce-Specific Notes
- **Amazon DSO ≈ 14-21 days** (14-day disbursement cycle + reserves)
- **Shopify DSO ≈ 2-5 days** (payment processor deposits)
- **DPO depends on supplier terms:** 30% deposit + 70% at ship = effectively DPO of 0-30 days
- **DIO is the biggest variable** — and the one you can actually control

### CCC Interpretation

| CCC (days) | Assessment | What It Means |
|------------|-----------|---------------|
| < 45 | Excellent | Cash cycles fast. Can self-fund growth. |
| 45-75 | Good | Manageable with planning. |
| 75-120 | Concerning | Cash drag is real. Requires careful management. |
| 120-180 | Dangerous | Business needs external capital or inventory reduction. |
| > 180 | Critical | Cash is trapped. Emergency inventory liquidation needed. |

### CCC by Channel

| Component | Amazon | Shopify | Hybrid |
|-----------|--------|---------|--------|
| DIO | 60-120 days | 45-90 days | Weighted average |
| DSO | 14-21 days | 2-5 days | Weighted average |
| DPO | 0-30 days | 0-30 days | Same supplier terms |
| **Typical CCC** | **75-130 days** | **30-75 days** | **Depends on mix** |

### CCC Per Product
Calculate CCC at the SKU level to identify which products trap cash longest:
```
SKU CCC = (Units in Stock ÷ Monthly Units Sold × 30) + Channel DSO − DPO
```

Highest CCC SKUs are prime candidates for promotion or liquidation.

## 6. EOQ Formula & Joint Replenishment

### Classic EOQ
```
EOQ = √(2 × D × S ÷ H)

Where:
  D = Annual demand (units)
  S = Order cost (shipping, customs, inspection per order)
  H = Holding cost per unit per year
    = Unit cost × (storage rate + capital cost + obsolescence risk)
    ≈ Unit cost × 25-35% for Amazon FBA sellers
```

### Practical EOQ Example
```
Product: Kitchen gadget
  Annual demand: 3,600 units (300/month)
  Order cost: $1,500 (ocean freight, customs, inspection)
  Unit cost: $8.00
  Holding cost: $8 × 30% = $2.40/unit/year

  EOQ = √(2 × 3600 × 1500 ÷ 2.40)
      = √(4,500,000)
      = 2,121 units
      = ~7 months supply

  Reorder cycle: 2121 ÷ 300 = ~7 months
  Orders per year: 3600 ÷ 2121 = ~1.7 orders/year
```

### Joint Replenishment Framework

**Problem:** Multiple SKUs from the same supplier. Standard EOQ assumes one SKU,
one supplier. Reality = shared shipping, different velocities, MOQ constraints.

**Framework:**
1. **Identify anchor SKU** — fastest seller from the supplier
2. **Set anchor reorder cycle** based on anchor's EOQ or practical cadence (typically 60-90 days)
3. **For each secondary SKU, at anchor cadence:**

```
Units Needed = Daily Velocity × Reorder Cycle Days × Safety Factor (1.2-1.5)

If Units Needed ≥ MOQ:
  → Order Units Needed. Normal.

If Units Needed < MOQ:
  → Option A: Order MOQ anyway
    Calculate excess holding cost: (MOQ − Needed) × Holding Cost per Unit × Excess Months
  → Option B: Skip this cycle, double next time
    Calculate stockout risk: P(stockout) × Lost Margin × Days Out
  → Option C: Separate shipment at higher per-unit shipping
    Calculate incremental shipping cost
  → Option D: Kill threshold check
    If optimal reorder cycle > 365 days → SKUkiller candidate

Choose lowest total cost option.
```

4. **Cycle Multiples Matrix:**

| SKU | Velocity | EOQ Cycle | Assigned Cycle | Multiple |
|-----|----------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| A (anchor) | 300/mo | 60 days | 60 days | 1× |
| B | 150/mo | 90 days | 60 days | 1× (slight overstock acceptable) |
| C | 50/mo | 150 days | 120 days | 2× (skip every other) |
| D | 10/mo | 400 days | 180 days | 3× (every third cycle) |
| E | 3/mo | 900 days | **KILL** | N/A — candidate for discontinuation |

## 7. Break-Even Formula

### Revenue Break-Even
```
Break-Even Revenue = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin %

Example:
  Fixed costs: $8,000/month
  CM%: 25%
  Break-even: $8,000 ÷ 0.25 = $32,000/month
```

### The Leverage Insight
> "Cutting $3K in OpEx can be worth more than $32K in new sales."

```
$3,000 cost cut at 25% CM → saves the equivalent of $12,000 in revenue
$3,000 revenue increase at 25% CM → adds $750 profit

The cost cut is 16× more powerful than the revenue increase.
```

### Per-SKU Break-Even
```
SKU Break-Even Units = Allocated Fixed Costs ÷ CM per Unit

If a SKU can't hit break-even units within a reasonable timeframe (90 days
for established, 180 days for new), it needs intervention or removal.
```

## 8. Contribution Margin Per SKU Calculation

### Amazon
```
CM/Unit = Sale Price
        − Referral Commission (typically 15%)
        − FBA Fulfillment Fee (fixed $ per unit by size/weight)
        − Storage Fee (allocated per unit per month)
        − COGS (landed: product + freight + duty + prep)
        − Ad Spend per Unit (total SKU ad spend ÷ units sold)

CM% = CM/Unit ÷ Sale Price
```

### Shopify
```
CM/Unit = Sale Price
        − Payment Processing (typically 2.9% + $0.30)
        − Shipping Cost (net of customer-paid shipping)
        − COGS (landed)
        − 3PL Pick/Pack Fee
        − Ad Spend per Unit (allocated from channel spend)

CM% = CM/Unit ÷ Sale Price
```

### Worked Example (Amazon)
```
Product: Bamboo cutting board
  Sale price: $34.99
  Referral commission: $5.25 (15%)
  FBA fulfillment: $6.75 (standard oversized)
  Storage fee: $0.15/month (allocated)
  COGS (landed): $7.50
  Ad spend: $2,100/month ÷ 420 units = $5.00/unit

  CM/Unit = $34.99 − $5.25 − $6.75 − $0.15 − $7.50 − $5.00
          = $10.34/unit

  CM% = $10.34 ÷ $34.99 = 29.6%

  Assessment: Acceptable. Watch fulfillment fee (19.3% of price — approaching danger zone).
  Diagnostic: If velocity is high, explore price increase to improve CM%.
```

## 9. Cash Floor Calculation

### Formula
```
Hard Floor = (Monthly Fixed Costs × 2) + Next Inventory PO Deposit (30%)
Soft Floor = Hard Floor × 1.15 to 1.25
```

### Example
```
Monthly fixed costs: $12,000
Next PO total: $40,000 (deposit = $12,000)
Tax reserve needed: $5,000

Hard Floor = ($12,000 × 2) + $12,000 = $36,000
Soft Floor = $36,000 × 1.20 = $43,200

Distribution Rule:
  Cash balance today: $65,000
  Max distribution = $65,000 − $43,200 = $21,800
  Recommended: $20,000 (round down, maintain buffer)
```

### Monthly Distribution Formula
```
Safe Distribution = (Avg Monthly Cash Inflow
                   − Fixed Costs
                   − Tax Reserve Allocation
                   − Inventory Reserve)
                   × 0.90

Never let post-distribution balance fall below soft floor.
```

## 10. IRR / XIRR Methodology

### Concept
IRR (Internal Rate of Return) measures the annualized return on invested capital.
For ecommerce businesses, use it to compare:
- Holding the business (earning profits) vs selling (lump sum)
- Buyer's perspective (what purchase price yields their target 30% IRR)

### Hold vs Sell Calculation
```
Hold scenario (XIRR inputs):
  Year 0: Initial capital invested (negative)
  Year 1-N: Annual distributions (positive)
  Year N: Terminal value estimate (positive)

Sell scenario:
  Year 0: Sale proceeds − taxes − broker fees
  Year 0+: Alternative investment returns

Compare IRRs. If Hold IRR > Sell IRR, keep running.
```

### Buyer's Perspective
```
Buyer targets 30% IRR over 3-5 year hold.

Purchase price that yields 30% IRR:
  Simple estimate: ~2.5-4× TTM SDE/profit
  Precise: XIRR of purchase price (negative) + monthly cash flows + exit value = 30%

Factors that increase multiple:
  − High growth rate
  − Diversified revenue (multi-channel, multi-product)
  − Low owner dependency
  − Strong brand / repeat customers
  − Clean financials

Factors that decrease multiple:
  − Single channel (Amazon only)
  − Declining growth
  − High working capital needs
  − Customer concentration
  − Owner-dependent operations
```

### Working Capital at Close
Buyers adjust purchase price for working capital:
```
Adjusted Price = Enterprise Value − Excess Working Capital Needed
```

If the business requires $500K of inventory to operate and the buyer inherits it,
that's $500K of their capital tied up before they earn a dollar of profit.

## 11. Working Capital Drag Formula

```
Working Capital Drag = (CCC ÷ 365) × Annual COGS

Alternative: WC Drag % = (CCC ÷ 365) × (COGS % of Revenue)
```

### Example
```
Business A:
  CCC: 145 days
  Annual Revenue: $3.2M
  COGS: 25% of revenue = $800K
  WC Drag = (145 ÷ 365) × $800K = $317K
  WC Drag % = (145 ÷ 365) × 25% = ~10% of revenue

Business B:
  CCC: 90 days
  Annual Revenue: $3.2M
  COGS: 40% of revenue = $1.28M
  WC Drag = (90 ÷ 365) × $1.28M = $315K
  WC Drag % = (90 ÷ 365) × 40% = ~10% of revenue

Same dollar drag, but Business A's CCC is 60% longer — offset by lower COGS %.
COGS% matters as much as CCC when evaluating working capital needs.
```

## 12. Returning Revenue Coverage Ratio

### Formula
```
RRCR = Returning Customer Revenue ÷ Monthly Operating Expenses

Where:
  Returning Customer Revenue = Total Revenue − New Customer Revenue
  Monthly Operating Expenses = All fixed and semi-fixed costs (team, software,
                               rent, insurance — NOT COGS or variable costs)
```

### Interpretation

| RRCR | Assessment | Meaning |
|------|-----------|---------|
| > 1.5 | Exceptional | Repeat customers fund operations with room to spare. New acquisition is pure growth investment. |
| 1.0 - 1.5 | Strong | Self-sustaining. OpEx covered by returning revenue. |
| 0.7 - 1.0 | Developing | Partially covered. Still dependent on new acquisition for operational funding. |
| < 0.7 | Fragile | Heavily dependent on new customer acquisition. If ads stop, business can't cover fixed costs. |

### Who Can Hit This
- **Consumable brands** (supplements, beauty, food): Best positioned — natural repeat behavior
- **Subscription/Subscribe & Save:** Built-in retention
- **Pet, baby, household:** Recurring need
- **Fashion, home decor, gifts:** Hardest — low natural repeat rate

> "If returning customer revenue covers your monthly OpEx, you're golden.
> New acquisition becomes pure pipeline growth."

## 13. Tax Rules of Thumb

### Reserve Calculation
```
Tax Reserve = MAX(
  20% of Contribution Margin,
  40% of Net Profit
)
```

### Entity Structure Quick Guide

| Net Profit | Recommendation | Why |
|-----------|---------------|-----|
| < $20K | LLC (default) | Not enough savings to justify S-corp complexity |
| $20K - $100K | S-Corp election | Saves ~15% SE tax on distributions above reasonable salary |
| > $100K | S-Corp (consult CPA) | Salary/distribution split optimization |

### S-Corp Split Example
```
Net profit: $150,000
Reasonable salary: $60,000

Without S-Corp (Schedule C):
  SE tax on $150K = ~$21,200

With S-Corp:
  SE tax on $60K salary = ~$9,180
  Distributions: $90K (no SE tax, still income tax)
  Savings: ~$12,000/year
```

## 14. Forecast Accuracy

### MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)
```
MAPE = (1/n) × Σ |Actual − Forecast| ÷ Actual × 100%
```

| MAPE | Assessment |
|------|-----------|
| < 10% | Excellent forecasting |
| 10-20% | Good — normal for ecommerce |
| 20-30% | Acceptable for volatile categories |
| > 30% | Forecasting methodology needs overhaul |

Track MAPE monthly. Persistent high MAPE means your driver assumptions are wrong.
